Canterbury’s Heritage Streetlights

Canterbury’s cast‑iron lighting columns are part of what makes the city’s streets distinctive. This atlas records every one, with its design, condition and location, as a shared picture to help plan their care, so that the Canterbury pattern continues to light the city for generations to come.

With thanks to Jenny and Allen MacDonald of Cossington Road, whose initial survey of over 800 photographs made this atlas possible.

About this atlas

What the map shows

Each dot is one street lighting column that has been photographed. Select a dot to see its photos, design, condition and asset number.

A white dot with a dashed ring is a column someone has reported that hasn't been checked yet. It joins the map once it has, so there's no need to report it again.

Asset numbers

Kent County Council gives every column an asset number, and each dot sits on KCC’s mapped position for that column.

The designs

  • Mather & Smith Biggleston Replica (A1) a replica of the Biggleston column, with the city’s CC monogram on the door
  • Biggleston Original (A2) a short framed door, with fluting all round the base
  • Sugg Biggleston Replica (A3) a Sugg replica with a crossbar and swan-neck lantern
  • Reproductions (B1, B2) modern heritage-style columns by DW Windsor
  • Reproduction Cylinder Drum (B3) a ribbed cylinder-drum column without the monogram, not a Biggleston pattern
  • Stubs and replacements shown where they now stand in place of an original

Good to know

The riverside path columns near Westwood Drive and Ambleside Place belong to Canterbury City Council, not KCC, so their numbers start with CCC and their dots sit where they were photographed.

Asset numbers come from KCC’s list of 26 September 2026 and may occasionally be wrong. Faces and number plates in the photos have been blurred.
